Time to Index · interactive

A search index that is wrong looks exactly like one that is fast.

Every retrieval benchmark scores a missing answer and a confidently outdated one the same way: zero. In production they are nothing alike. This page shows you the difference, then lets you change the scoring rule and watch the ranking turn upside down.

ABSENT

The API returns nothing relevant. Your agent retries, widens the query, or says it doesn't know. Annoying, and visible.

STALE

The API confidently returns the answer that was true yesterday. Your agent cites it — with no signal anywhere downstream that it's wrong.

Scored out of one, these are the same failure. They are not the same failure.

What the statistics actually record

An index seen ABSENT at 15 min and FRESH at 1 hour was indexed somewhere in between. The honest answer is a bracket, not a point.

This is why the benchmark uses survival analysis rather than averages — the same maths used for clinical trials where many patients outlive the study. Most facts are still un-indexed at the final rung.

What you just saw

Provider D answered instantly at every rung — and was wrong every time. It served version 7.2.4 with total confidence for three full days. Under conventional scoring, which asks only "did a plausible result come back," D looks like the fastest index in the test.

Provider A returned nothing for the first fifteen minutes. It scored a miss twice. But it never once handed over a wrong answer, and it had the correct one within the hour.

In production these are not close. An agent that gets nothing retries, widens its search, or tells the user it doesn't know. An agent that gets 7.2.4 cites it — in your product, to your customer, with no signal anywhere downstream that the number is a week old.

That is the entire argument. The distinction costs nothing to measure and no existing retrieval benchmark makes it.

Provider names are deliberately anonymous and the responses are hand-authored to illustrate four distinct failure modes. This page is evidence about how the instrument reasons, not a measurement of any company's product — the same convention the project's own tti demo follows. The real benchmark probes at t+5m, 15m, 1h, 6h, 24h and 72h against publishers that timestamp their own facts, and publishes raw responses alongside every figure.
